Mouse Removal

Mouse removal is about clearing an active group completely, because mice breed rapidly and a pair left behind can rebuild numbers within weeks. Murvo begins by confirming where mice nest and feed, checking attics, wall voids, crawl spaces, and behind kitchen cabinets. Removal sits within our broader mice control programs and general rodent removal service, giving you trapping, cleanup, and sealing as one coordinated job. You can review all the connected services on our services page before you book a visit. We work room by room so no nesting site is missed, checking warm spots near boilers, appliances, and pipe runs where mice love to settle, raise young, and stay hidden during daylight hours.

Technicians set multiple traps at proven activity points, return to clear and reset them, and track catch numbers until they fall to zero. We then arrange rodent waste cleanup for droppings and nesting debris, and seal access points as part of proper rodent exclusion so the route indoors is closed permanently. Dead mice in inaccessible voids are located and dealt with before odor becomes a problem. For urgent help today, contact Murvo for a same-day response from our team. All removed animals and contaminated material are bagged and taken away, and we advise on safe cleaning of cabinets and surfaces, so your kitchen and storage areas feel properly yours again afterwards.

For cost context, Angi's 2026 national data reports rat extermination at $396 on average, typically $189 to $655, inspections from $100 to $200, and exclusion from $200 to $600. Mouse work is priced by Murvo as a flat rate quoted in writing after assessment, reflecting the number of rooms and amount of sealing involved. We favor trap-based, no-poison removal that is safer around children and pets, use integrated pest management throughout, and back the finished job with our written workmanship guarantee. For homes with repeated problems, seasonal rodent proofing adds an extra layer of defense, checking sweeps, screens, and seals before the fall rush of mice seeking indoor warmth begins.
