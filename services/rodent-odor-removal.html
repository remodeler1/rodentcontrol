Rodent Odor Removal

Rodent odors have three usual sources: urine soaked into insulation and timber, scent-marked nesting material, and — worst of all — a dead rodent decomposing in a wall void or attic. Sprays and plug-ins only mask these smells, and the odor returns within days because the source is still there. Murvo's rodent odor removal is built on one principle: source removal before deodorizing, always. If the smell is sudden, strong and localized, a carcass is likely, and our dead rodent removal team will locate and extract it. Longer-standing musty smells usually trace back to contamination needing rodent waste cleanup.

We start by tracing the odor to its strongest point using inspection access, moisture patterns and knowledge of rodent travel routes, including wall rodent removal techniques for void spaces. The source material is removed, the area receives rodent disinfection, and only then do we deodorize with treatments that neutralize odor molecules rather than perfuming over them. Where droppings accompany the smell, rodent dropping cleanup is completed first. Severely soaked insulation is replaced, because no surface treatment can rescue it. Finally, the entry points are sealed so new rodents cannot recreate the problem, and you can review the full remediation range on our services page.

Odor work is priced as a flat rate quoted in writing once we know the source — a single carcass extraction costs far less than replacing an attic of soaked insulation. For general context, Angi's 2026 national data puts rat extermination at $396 on average, typically $189–$655, and inspections at $100–$200, which is the stage that pinpoints your odor source. Our written workmanship guarantee covers the result: if the identified source was removed and the smell persists as described, we return to investigate. Living with a smell you cannot locate is miserable — contact Murvo and let us find it properly.
