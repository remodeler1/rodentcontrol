Rodent Disinfection

Removing rodents is only half the job. Their droppings, urine and body oils leave behind bacteria, potential viruses and pheromone scent trails that actively attract new rodents to the same routes and nesting spots. Rodent disinfection treats those contaminated zones directly: attics, crawl spaces, wall voids, garages and kitchen voids where activity was found. It matters most where children crawl, food is stored or pets roam. Disinfection normally follows rodent waste cleanup, because disinfectant works properly on surfaces once bulk waste is safely removed, and it pairs with rodent dropping cleanup in living spaces.

Our process applies a disinfectant treatment to all identified contamination points, observing proper dwell time so the product can work, with technicians in gloves and respiratory protection — the same CDC-style caution we apply to any droppings: disinfect first, never disturb dry waste, and never dry-sweep. Treated scent trails are neutralized as part of the application, supporting the rodent exclusion work that physically blocks re-entry. Where odor persists after sanitizing, rodent odor removal targets what remains. You can see how disinfection fits the wider program on our services page, alongside rodent control plans.

Disinfection is quoted as a flat rate in writing, scaled to the number and size of areas treated rather than sold as a vague add-on. For wider cost context, Angi's 2026 national figures average rat extermination at $396, usually $189–$655, with inspections between $100 and $200 — disinfection is normally a modest part of a full remediation program. We select products with household safety in mind and offer pet-safe options, advising you exactly when treated areas can be reoccupied. All work is covered by our written workmanship guarantee. To schedule disinfection after a removal, contact Murvo and tell us where activity was found.
