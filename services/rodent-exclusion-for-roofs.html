Rodent Exclusion for Roofs

The roof is the most overlooked rodent entry point in the home. Rats scale drainpipes, brickwork and overhanging branches, then slip through gaps at the eaves, broken ridge vents, lifted tiles and unsealed pipe penetrations — a rat needs a gap of barely half an inch. Once inside, the attic offers warmth, nesting material and safe routes down into wall voids. Murvo's rodent exclusion for roofs begins with a full roofline survey, usually alongside attic rodent removal, identifying every opening and the rub marks and droppings that show which ones rodents actually use. It forms the roof element of our wider rodent exclusion program.

Sealing uses gnaw-resistant materials chosen for each opening: galvanized mesh and metal flashing at eaves and ridges, proper covers for vent openings, and sealed collars where pipes and cables pierce the roof. Foam alone is never the answer — rodents chew straight through it. Roof work is coordinated with rodent exclusion for vents, since roof and gable vents are favorite entry routes, and with rodent exclusion for chimneys where a chimney stack meets the roofline. Together with general rodent proofing at ground level, this creates a complete sealed envelope, detailed on our services page.

Roof exclusion is quoted as a flat rate in writing after the survey, itemized by opening so you can see exactly what is being sealed. For context, Angi's 2026 national data puts exclusion work at $200–$600 typically, with rat extermination averaging $396, usually $189–$655 — roof sealing sits sensibly within that exclusion range for most homes. Materials are fitted to shed water correctly and respect your roof's existing construction, and the work carries our written workmanship guarantee. If rodents return through a point we sealed, we return to fix it. Book your roofline survey by contacting Murvo today.
