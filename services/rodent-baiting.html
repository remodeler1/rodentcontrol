Rodent Baiting

Safety comes first with Murvo's rodent baiting. We never scatter loose poison: bait is placed only inside tamper-resistant, locked stations that children and pets cannot open, positioned along runways, fence lines and entry points after a full rodent inspection. Signs you may need baiting include droppings that keep returning, gnaw marks, night scratching and sightings despite traps. Baiting suits established rat activity outdoors or in garages, but it is never our only step. We pair it with rodent trapping and rodent exclusion so new rodents cannot simply replace those removed.

Our process follows Inspect, Remove, Exclude, Clean and Monitor. Background-checked technicians map activity, choose station locations away from pet play areas and food, label and secure every station, then return to check consumption and adjust placement. Where baiting is not appropriate indoors, we recommend rodent removal by trapping first, followed by rodent proofing of gaps around pipes, vents and doors. You can review all options on our rodent services page or contact Murvo to discuss pet and child concerns before any station is placed on your property. We also photograph each station location for your records and explain exactly what to watch for between our scheduled monitoring visits.

For cost guidance, Angi's 2026 national data puts rat extermination at $396 on average, typically $189–$655, inspections $100–$200 and exclusion $200–$600; Murvo instead quotes one flat rate in writing before work begins. State rules matter: California limits anticoagulant rodenticides, and we follow those restrictions and local regulations everywhere. Our exclusion-first IPM approach reduces reliance on bait over time, stations are monitored and removed when activity ends, and every job is backed by our written workmanship guarantee and licensed, insured service across the United States. That careful record-keeping helps us prove when activity has genuinely ended, rather than guessing and removing stations too early.
