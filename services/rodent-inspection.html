Rodent Inspection

A rodent inspection is the essential first step when you hear movement in the attic, find droppings, or spot a single rat or mouse and want to know the full extent of the problem. Murvo technicians examine foundations, siding, pipe penetrations, garage doors, and roof junctions, because mice can squeeze through a gap the width of a pencil. Inside, we check insulation, stored items, and food areas, and our findings guide whether you need complete rodent control or a focused fix, all explained through our services overview. We also listen carefully to what you have heard and seen, because the timing and location of noises often point directly towards the most active nesting areas.

During the visit we document active runways, nesting material, gnaw damage, and contamination, taking photos so you can see exactly what we found. If animals are present, we outline strategic rodent trapping and safe rodent removal options, followed by roof exclusion work or vent and chimney sealing where needed. You receive recommendations in plain language with priorities marked urgent or preventive. To book a visit at a time that suits you, simply contact our team for a prompt, same-day response nationwide. That level of detail prevents wasted spending on treatments aimed at the wrong species or the wrong part of the building, which is a common and costly mistake.

On cost, Angi's 2026 national figures place inspections at $100 to $200, while full rat extermination averages $396, typically $189 to $655, and exclusion work runs $200 to $600. Those are national benchmarks, not Murvo prices: after your inspection we provide a flat-rate quote in writing for any recommended work, so you decide with full information. Our inspections support an integrated approach, favoring preventive rodent proofing and durable rodent exclusion over repeated treatments, backed by our written workmanship guarantee. A clear inspection also helps you plan ahead, tackling urgent sealing now and scheduling preventive work before colder weather drives more rodents towards warm indoor shelter.
